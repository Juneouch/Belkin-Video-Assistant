# Belkin Product Commercial Directing Framework

Use this as a compact director's reference. It is a planning heuristic, not a substitute for the approved treatment, product truth, or audience research.

## PDP commercial logic

A product-detail-page film sits close to a purchase decision. Direct it to answer, in order:

1. What is this and why should I notice it?
2. What problem or desire does it address?
3. How does it work in a real or believable context?
4. What visible proof makes the benefit credible?
5. Why this product rather than a generic alternative?

Commerce viewers may encounter the film in a thumbnail, autoplay preview, muted state, mobile crop, or interrupted session. Build a silent-first visual spine: product identity, action, cause/effect, and key proof must survive without narration. Use captions/supers for essential claims when the brief allows them. Audio should add tactility, pace, and brand memory rather than carrying the only explanation.

The film can still have a strong advertising point of view. Use a memorable hook, a clear emotional turn, and a polished hero ending, but do not let atmosphere hide the product or make the benefit inferential.

## Shot size as emotional and informational grammar

Shot labels vary by production culture; use the label plus a plain-language frame description.

| Scale | Primary job | Product use |
|---|---|---|
| EWS/WS | Place the user/product in a world; show relationship and consequence | Context, ecosystem, lifestyle payoff |
| MWS/FS | Show body language and complete action | Interaction, setup, handoff, use case |
| MS/MCU | Balance behavior and expression | Human response, decision, before/after |
| CU | Create intimacy and attention | Product in hand, interface, connection, tactile proof |
| ECU/macro/detail | Make design or performance undeniable | Port, connector, texture, seam, indicator, material response |
| Hero/packshot | Create recall and purchase confidence | Clean silhouette, variant family, logo, CTA |

Treat shot size as a change in viewer proximity: public/contextual, social/behavioral, personal/intimate, then forensic/proof. Do not use a closeup merely because it looks expensive; give it a specific information or emotional job.

## Film aesthetic and camera language

Define the aesthetic with controllable variables rather than adjectives alone:

- **Lens/FOV:** wide lenses create spatial energy and show environment; normal lenses feel legible and natural; longer lenses compress space and isolate the product.
- **Perspective/height:** low angle can confer capability or heroism; eye level creates trust; top-down can clarify layout and system relationships; oblique angles can create tension but should be used sparingly for precision products.
- **Movement:** push-in = discovery/importance; pull-back = reveal/context; lateral track = comparison/progression; orbit = form/material exploration; follow = lived use; locked-off = confidence/control; handheld = human immediacy or instability.
- **Depth of field:** shallow focus creates desire and intimacy; deeper focus supports proof and spatial comprehension. Never blur the feature that must be read.
- **Edit rhythm:** low arousal favors longer holds, fewer moves, and clean transitions; high arousal favors shorter shots, kinetic reframes, impact cuts, and motivated acceleration. Keep a readable hero hold even in fast films.
- **Texture/aesthetic:** choose a coherent visual surface—clean studio precision, warm domestic realism, tactile lifestyle, graphic futurism, premium macro, or kinetic tech—and name its contrast, grain, saturation, camera movement, and light behavior.

## Valence / arousal and cinematic mood mapping

Use Russell's circumplex as a two-axis design tool:

- **Valence:** unpleasant/pleasant, threat/comfort, friction/delight.
- **Arousal:** activated/quiet, urgent/settled, kinetic/still.

Map the treatment's emotional arc before designing shots. A practical quadrant map:

| Target state | Directing choices |
|---|---|
| Pleasant + low arousal: comfort, calm, trust | Soft or broad sources, gentle contrast, warm/neutral palette, stable frames, slow dolly, longer holds, rounded compositions, quiet tactile sound |
| Pleasant + high arousal: excitement, delight, breakthrough | Brighter accents, crisp edge light, saturated but controlled color, dynamic push/orbit, graphic diagonals, rhythmic cuts, sync hits and lift |
| Unpleasant + high arousal: friction, urgency, overload | Harder contrast, constrained framing, cooler or contaminated color, restless motion, occlusion, sharper transients; resolve visibly through the product |
| Unpleasant + low arousal: fatigue, dullness, stuckness | Flat or low-energy rhythm, desaturated palette, static or distant frame, low contrast or heavy negative space; use the product as the visual release |

Always design the turn: the product should change either valence, arousal, or both. Example: chaotic/high-arousal negative friction becomes calm/high-valence control, then resolves into calm confidence or elevated delight.

Do not claim that a color, lens, or shot size guarantees an emotion. Mood emerges from the combination of story context, performance, sound, edit, light, color, and viewer interpretation.

## Composition and visual design

Build a hierarchy in every frame:

1. primary subject/product or the action that proves the benefit;
2. secondary human/prop/environmental context;
3. tertiary texture, color accent, or graphic support.

Use negative space for copy and reframing. Keep the product silhouette readable against the background. Use leading lines, contrast, scale, repetition, symmetry/asymmetry, and foreground occlusion intentionally. Establish screen direction and eye-lines before cutting between live action and CG. For mobile crops, protect the product and hand action inside a center-safe area unless the brief requires edge composition.

For CG, composition is also lighting design: reflection cards, gradients, and dark negative fill can define edge shape more precisely than a brighter key. Design the background to support the product's hue and finish; avoid a background with equal contrast or equal visual noise.

## Lighting and material on CG

Start with the material truth, then choose light that makes it legible:

- **Diffuse/soft-touch plastics:** broad, soft gradients and controlled edge highlights reveal curvature without making the surface look wet.
- **Gloss plastics/lacquer:** larger reflection cards, clean gradients, and carefully placed highlights; protect logo and UI from blown reflections.
- **Brushed/anodized metal:** long directional highlights, subtle roughness variation, edge separation, and a believable environment to reflect.
- **Glass/translucent parts:** visible background/refraction structure, rim or backlight, controlled thickness, and a readable boundary; avoid empty transparency.
- **Rubber/fabric/paper:** grazing light and microtexture; keep enough contrast to distinguish tactile finish.
- **Screens/LEDs:** exposure discipline, readable UI/indicator state, bloom kept subordinate to information.

In a physically based workflow, `base color`, `metallic`, `roughness`, `IOR/transmission`, `normal`, and optional coat/sheens describe different material behaviors. Roughness is especially important: it controls highlight sharpness and perceived finish. Do not fix a weak model by making every surface metallic or mirror-smooth.

Use a lighting plan with key, fill/negative fill, rim or separation, environment/reflection cards, and practical/emissive sources. State what each source reveals. For a hero shot, specify the desired highlight path, shadow density, floor contact, reflection cleanliness, and background falloff.

## Audio-visual language

Design sound in parallel with picture:

- use sonic motifs for connection, charging, click, magnetic snap, airflow, or UI confirmation when they are truthful;
- synchronize tactile sound with visible contact and motion;
- use silence or reduced density to create a before/after contrast;
- let music tempo and cut rhythm support the valence/arousal trajectory;
- write VO only where it clarifies something the image cannot prove efficiently.

Mark whether audio is `diegetic`, `designed product SFX`, `music`, `VO`, or `silence`. In the shotlist, state the visual proof even if the sound cue is removed.
